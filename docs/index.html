Can an AI trade?

A paper-trading research desk for Nasdaq and S&P futures. Every rule was written down before it was tested, checked against ten years of data it had never seen, and the failures are published next to the wins. Rules freeze on 31 December; the real test starts on 1 January.

The book

Four intraday momentum strategies, one account, 1% risk per trade, and any trade whose costs eat more than 10% of the risk is skipped.

The honest reading: these edges only show up in the more volatile markets since 2018. In the calm years of 2011–2017 the book lost money. Research on intraday momentum finds the same thing: it works when volatility is high and options dealers are short gamma, and fades when markets are calm. So Argon v2 is a bet that today’s market regime continues. Live results will likely be well below the backtest.

Method

How each claim was earned.

  1. Pre-registeredPass marks, test windows and every adjustment were committed to GitHub before any test ran. The commit timestamps are the proof.
  2. Real costsRound-trip costs on every trade, extra stop slippage in the first 15 minutes, limit orders that only fill if price trades through, roll days and FOMC afternoons skipped, then everything re-run at double costs.
  3. Walk-forwardStrategies that re-pick settings only ever use months that have already happened.
  4. Round 1109 adjustments tested; 1 survived and passed its lockbox (QuantLab skipping FOMC days).
  5. Round 217 adjustments after a council review; 2 survived (risk parity). Then the base book was tested on 2011–2020 and failed.
  6. Bugs foundA daily loss limit that looked good only because of lookahead; roll days that were never actually skipped; a missing 2010–2020 FOMC calendar; September 2026 counted twice. All fixed and published.
  7. FreezeFrom 31 Dec 23:59 UTC nothing changes except an emergency kill switch. Every change before then is logged in PREREGISTRATION.md.

The AI desk

Every morning before the open, an AI forecasts whether today will be a trend day or a chop day, and picks a size for the book.

It runs in the shadow next to two other arms: the plain book at 1×, and a one-line rule (size by yesterday’s range). The AI only gets real control after 120 trading days, and only if it beats both by 0.15 Sharpe. Same model, frozen prompt, middle answer of three runs. 0 days scored so far.

What failed

Most ideas didn’t survive. Here are the big ones.

  • The original “liquidity sweep” strategy (ICT)+40.8R → −16.3RSweep of a level, structure shift, fair value gap. Profitable until realistic costs and slippage were added. Still runs in the old dashboard as a separate AI experiment.
  • 15+ influencer strategiesall lostCRT, “sweeping inverse” FVGs, a $328k ICT model, dip-buying RSI. Lesson: following the push works on the Nasdaq; fading it loses after costs.
  • A daily loss limitlookahead bugLooked like +0.09 Sharpe; with exact trade times it made results worse.
  • The v2 book on 2011–2020Sharpe −1.03Ten never-seen years, opened once. Across all their trades, three of four strategies had a small edge before costs and QuantLab had none. Adding the cost rule afterwards lifted it to 0.12, still a loss ($92,695 from $100,000), and that fix was chosen after seeing the result. Published anyway.
